Pleural Effusions

A pleural effusion is an abnormal accumulation of fluid in the pleural space — the area between the lung and the chest wall. It is a symptom of an underlying condition rather than a disease itself.

What is a Pleural Effusion?

Normally, a small amount of fluid lubricates the pleural space. A pleural effusion develops when fluid accumulates abnormally — either because too much is produced or too little is absorbed. Effusions can be classified as transudates (caused by systemic factors like heart failure) or exudates (caused by local disease such as infection or cancer).

Common Causes

  • Heart failure: Most common cause of transudative effusion
  • Pneumonia (parapneumonic effusion): Most common cause of exudative effusion
  • Malignancy: Lung, breast, lymphoma, and mesothelioma
  • Pulmonary embolism
  • Liver cirrhosis (hepatic hydrothorax)
  • Kidney disease (nephrotic syndrome)
  • Tuberculosis
  • Connective tissue diseases (lupus, rheumatoid arthritis)

Symptoms

  • Shortness of breath, especially when lying flat
  • Pleuritic chest pain (sharp pain worsened by breathing)
  • Dry cough
  • Reduced exercise tolerance
  • Symptoms of the underlying cause (e.g., fever in infection)

Diagnosis

Chest X-ray is usually the first imaging test, but ultrasound is now widely used for both diagnosis and guiding drainage procedures. CT of the chest provides detailed information about the pleural space and surrounding structures.

Thoracentesis (needle aspiration of pleural fluid) is often performed to obtain fluid for analysis — protein levels, LDH, glucose, cell count, and cultures help determine the cause.

Treatment

Treatment targets the underlying cause. Specific interventions for the effusion itself include:

  • Therapeutic thoracentesis: Needle drainage to relieve breathlessness
  • Chest tube / intercostal drain: For larger or complicated (infected) effusions
  • Indwelling pleural catheter (IPC): For recurrent malignant effusions — allows regular home drainage
  • Pleurodesis: Sealing of the pleural space to prevent recurrence in malignant effusions
  • Video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery (VATS): For loculated or complex effusions

Key Facts

  • 1 million+ cases/year in North America
  • Ultrasound guides safe drainage
  • Underlying cause must be treated
  • Malignant effusions often recur
