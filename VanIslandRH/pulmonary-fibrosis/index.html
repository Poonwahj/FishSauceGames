Pulmonary Fibrosis & ILD

Pulmonary fibrosis is a condition in which lung tissue becomes scarred and thickened, making it progressively more difficult to breathe. It falls under the broader category of interstitial lung diseases (ILD).

What is Pulmonary Fibrosis?

In pulmonary fibrosis, the delicate lung tissue becomes damaged, inflamed, and then replaced with scar (fibrotic) tissue. This thickening stiffens the lungs and reduces the ability to transfer oxygen into the bloodstream. The most common and severe form is Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis (IPF), where no identifiable cause is found.

Causes

Pulmonary fibrosis can result from a variety of causes:

  • Idiopathic (IPF): Unknown cause; most common form in adults over 60
  • Connective tissue diseases: Rheumatoid arthritis, scleroderma, lupus
  • Hypersensitivity pneumonitis: Inhaled organic dusts (bird feathers, mould)
  • Occupational exposures: Asbestos, silica, coal dust
  • Medications: Certain chemotherapy agents, amiodarone, nitrofurantoin
  • Radiation therapy to the chest

Symptoms

  • Progressive breathlessness, initially with exertion, later at rest
  • Dry, persistent cough
  • Fatigue
  • Clubbing of the fingers (in some patients)
  • "Velcro-like" crackling sounds heard on chest auscultation

Diagnosis

Diagnosis involves a combination of high-resolution CT (HRCT) of the chest, pulmonary function tests, and sometimes surgical lung biopsy. A multidisciplinary team discussion including respirologists, radiologists, and pathologists is considered the gold standard for diagnosis.

HRCT findings are central to diagnosis — the pattern and distribution of fibrosis guides the diagnostic classification and treatment decisions.

Treatment

Treatment depends on the specific type of ILD:

  • Antifibrotic medications (nintedanib, pirfenidone): For IPF — slow the rate of fibrosis progression
  • Immunosuppression: For inflammatory ILDs (e.g., those associated with connective tissue disease)
  • Oxygen therapy: For patients with low oxygen levels
  • Pulmonary rehabilitation: Improves exercise capacity and quality of life
  • Lung transplantation: For select patients with advanced disease

Key Facts

  • IPF most common in adults over 60
  • Men affected more than women
  • HRCT is key to diagnosis
  • Early referral improves outcomes
